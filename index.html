Do you know how much zakat you owe?

Mizan is a free zakat calculator for people who budget in Lunch Money or YNAB. It works out your zakat from your real balances, not a guess, and shows how every amount adds up.

What happens to your data.

Mizan does the maths in your browser. It sends your token only to Lunch Money or YNAB, makes read-only requests, and never saves your token or balances. Only your method choices are remembered on this device.

Why zakat matters.

For Muslims whose wealth reaches the nisab and is held for a lunar year, giving 2.5% of it is a duty, owed to the people Allah names in the Quran.

The three rules behind the number.

The rate: 2.5%, one fortieth

2.5% of your whole eligible wealth after debts, not just the part above the nisab.

The nisab: the minimum you must own

The value of 85 g of gold or 595 g of silver. Below it, no zakat is due. Scholars differ on which metal to use.

The hawl: a full lunar year

Your wealth has to be held for twelve lunar months (354 days) before zakat on it is due.

Connect your account.

Use a Lunch Money access token (a private code you create in its settings) or YNAB’s own sign-in. Either way, Mizan only reads and never changes anything.

Not ready to connect? first. They are made up.

Where are your balances?

Sign in on Lunch Money’s own site and pick the budget to read. Mizan only gets permission to read, so it can’t change anything.

You’ll approve on Lunch Money’s site, then come straight back here to review your accounts.

Or use an access token instead:

  1. 1
    Create an access token in Lunch Money

    In Lunch Money, go to Settings → Developers and request a new access token. Name it “Mizan”. Step-by-step guide↗ (opens in a new tab)

  2. 2
    Paste it here

    Next you’ll see your accounts and choose which ones to count.

  3. 3
    Switch the token off when you’re done

    A Lunch Money token can change data as well as read it. Mizan only ever reads, but once you have your estimate, go back to Settings → Developers and choose Revoke for the “Mizan” token.

Sign in on YNAB’s own site and allow read-only access. There’s nothing to make or switch off afterwards.

You’ll approve on YNAB’s site, then come straight back here to review your accounts.

  • Read-only. YNAB refuses any change, so Mizan can only look.
  • Short-lived. YNAB ends the access by itself after 2 hours.
  • One plan. If you have several YNAB plans, Mizan reads the one you changed most recently.

What happens to your data

  • Your token goes straight from your browser to Lunch Money, and nowhere else. The person who made Mizan never sees it.
  • Mizan only looks up your accounts and balances. It never adds, edits or deletes anything.
  • The box is emptied as soon as you choose Connect my accounts, and the token is never saved.
  • If you sign in instead, Lunch Money gives Mizan read-only access to the budget you pick. It’s kept only in memory, never saved, and ended as soon as your balances are read. You can remove Mizan from Connected apps in Lunch Money at any time.
  • You sign in on YNAB’s site. Mizan never sees your password.
  • YNAB gives Mizan a temporary access token. It’s removed from the address bar at once, kept only in memory, and never saved.
  • Your balances and transactions are never saved or shared.
